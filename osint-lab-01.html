Investigation Procedure

01
Requirement Intake & Scoping
Defined exactly what the client needed before opening a single source: identity confirmation, personal legal exposure, corporate risk posture, and reputational considerations. Scoped the engagement to open-source and public-record methods only, consistent with the client's constraints.
02
Identity & Corporate Registry Confirmation
Confirmed the subject's current role and the target company's legal structure through Secretary of State / corporate registry search — incorporation date, state of incorporation, and officer-of-record status.
03
Systematic Source Development
Worked outward from confirmed identity through six additional source categories: Google dorking / advanced search operators, LinkedIn profile research, news and trade media search, court records search (state and federal portals), proprietary public records database queries, and broader social media review.
04
Source Documentation & Risk Categorization
Logged every finding as it was pulled — source, date, and category — and sorted results into personal record checks, corporate ownership/structure, and legal/reputational exposure. Negative findings were documented as explicit negative results, not left blank.
05
Report Delivery & Client Follow-Up
Delivered the full report, then re-reviewed it ahead of the client's follow-up window. Caught and corrected a leftover template line before the client's questions arrived, and responded to the client's follow-up email within the required 24-hour window, prepared to amend and re-date the report if requested.

// 04

Due Diligence Analysis

1
No personal derogatory findings
Across all eight personal-record categories, nothing was identified that would create personal legal or reputational exposure for the subject.
2
Cross-border ownership risk
Full ownership by a foreign parent holding company introduces regulatory and geopolitical considerations the client should factor into any U.S.-based business decision involving this company.
3
Operational stability signals
Recent and ongoing workforce reductions suggest continued strategic repositioning worth monitoring, not a closed issue.
4
Corporate reputational exposure
Litigation tied to a former sponsorship partnership and a prior workplace-related consent decree represent historical reputational considerations, even without personal exposure to the subject.
5
Recommended next steps
Monitor regulatory developments tied to foreign ownership, track ongoing litigation, conduct a financial posture review, and continue media/reputation monitoring — the parts of the picture open-source methods alone can flag but not close out.
